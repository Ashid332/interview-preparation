# Behavioral Interview Cheat Sheet

## Key Concepts
| Concept | Description |
|---|---|
| STAR Method | Situation, Task, Action, Result - standard format for answering behavioral questions. |
| Leadership Principles | Guiding values for companies (e.g., Amazon's 16 LPs) to assess cultural fit. |
| Impact | Quantifiable outcomes of your actions (e.g., increased revenue by 20%). |
| Ownership | Taking responsibility for outcomes, regardless of the hurdles. |

## Must-Know Items
- Prepare at least 5 versatile stories that can fit multiple behavioral questions.
- Always emphasize *your* specific role ("I did X"), rather than the team ("We did X").
- Be honest about failures, but focus on the learnings.
- Understand the company's core values before the interview.

## Common Interview Questions (Quick)
1. Tell me about a time you had a conflict with a coworker.
2. Describe a project that failed and what you learned.
3. How do you handle tight deadlines?
4. Tell me about a time you went above and beyond for a customer.

## Critical Commands/Patterns
*The STAR Pattern:*
- **S**ituation: Set the scene (briefly).
- **T**ask: What was the goal/problem?
- **A**ction: What steps did *you* take? (Core of the answer)
- **R**esult: What was the outcome? (Metrics matter)

## Decision Framework
- **Choosing a story:** Does it showcase growth? Does it involve cross-functional collaboration? Does it end with a positive or highly educational outcome?

## Common Mistakes
- Spending too much time on Situation/Task and not enough on Action/Result.
- Blaming others for failures.
- Not quantifying the impact (saying "it was successful" instead of "it reduced latency by 40%").
- Rambling without structure.

## One-Minute Review
- Remember: STAR method. Focus on "I". Highlight quantifiable impact. Show empathy, ownership, and a growth mindset. Have 5 core stories ready to adapt.
