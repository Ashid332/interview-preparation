# Communication Interview Scorecard

This rubric evaluates how effectively a candidate conveys information, listens to feedback, and tailors their message to their audience. Communication is assessed continuously across all interview rounds.

## Scoring Rubric

* **5 - Strong Hire:**
  * **Traits:** Extremely articulate, concise, and engaging.
  * **Evidence:** Can explain highly complex technical concepts to a non-technical audience (e.g., using excellent analogies). Actively listens, validates the interviewer's points, and structures their answers perfectly (e.g., "There are three main points here...").
* **4 - Hire:**
  * **Traits:** Clear and effective communicator. Easy to work with.
  * **Evidence:** Answers questions directly without rambling. Good active listening. Asks clarifying questions when unsure. Whiteboard/diagram communication is neat and logical.
* **3 - Neutral:**
  * **Traits:** Can get the point across, but requires effort from the listener.
  * **Evidence:** Rambles occasionally. Sometimes answers a different question than the one asked. Uses overly technical jargon when not necessary. Requires the interviewer to constantly steer the conversation back on track.
* **2 - Lean No Hire:**
  * **Traits:** Very difficult to follow. Poor listener.
  * **Evidence:** Frequent misunderstandings. Interrupts the interviewer constantly. Monotone or disengaged. Fails to pick up on obvious verbal cues or hints.
* **1 - Strong No Hire:**
  * **Traits:** Completely ineffective communication or highly disrespectful.
  * **Evidence:** Ignores the interviewer completely. Speaks condescendingly. Refuses to explain their thought process (e.g., "It's obvious why this works").

## Hiring Manager Notes

For the Hiring Committee:
- Communication is the backbone of remote and hybrid work environments.
- Distinguish between "nervousness" and "poor communication". A candidate who stumbles on words initially but clearly explains a complex algorithm is fine. A candidate who confidently talks over the interviewer is a red flag.
- A "5" in communication often covers for slight technical gaps, as it shows high coachability and team fit.
