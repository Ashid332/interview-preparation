# Senior Engineer Roadmap (L5+)

As a Senior Engineer, the expectations shift dramatically from "Can you write code?" to "Can you design systems, mentor others, and deliver complex projects?"

## Evaluation Criteria Matrix

| Area | Mid-Level Expectation | Senior Expectation |
|---|---|---|
| **Coding** | Fast, bug-free, optimal. | Solid, but edge-case awareness and clean code matter more than raw speed. |
| **System Design** | Knows components (LB, Cache, DB). | Drives trade-off discussions. Focuses on bottlenecks, cost, and maintainability. |
| **Leadership** | Manages own tasks well. | Mentors juniors, influences team direction, resolves technical deadlocks. |
| **Ambiguity** | Needs well-defined tickets. | Takes high-level business goals and creates the technical roadmap. |

## Preparation Focus

### 1. Advanced System Architecture (40% of Prep)
You will be judged heavily on your ability to design large-scale systems.
- **Deep Dives**: Don't just say "use Kafka." Explain *why* Kafka over RabbitMQ, how partitions work, and how you'd handle consumer failure.
- **Topics**: Microservices, Event-Driven Architecture, Consistency models (Eventual vs Strong), Database internals.

### 2. Behavioral and Leadership (40% of Prep)
Senior behavioral interviews are rigorous. You need concrete stories for:
- A time you fundamentally disagreed with a product manager or director, and how you handled it.
- A project that failed due to your technical oversight and what you learned.
- How you mentored an underperforming engineer.
- A time you had to make a critical technical decision with incomplete data.

### 3. Coding (20% of Prep)
While still required, the coding round is often more about code quality, modularity (OOD), and testing than tricky algorithms.
- Brush up on LeetCode Mediums.
- Practice writing production-ready code on a whiteboard (meaningful variable names, modular functions, error handling).

## Final Checklist Before Interview
- [ ] Prepared deep-dive explanations for the 2 most complex systems you've architected.
- [ ] Practiced driving a 45-minute system design interview (acting as the peer/leader, not a student).
- [ ] Documented 5 critical leadership stories focusing on impact and conflict resolution.
