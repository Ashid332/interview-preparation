# Offer Evaluation

Congratulations, you have an offer (or multiple). Now you have to make a decision. Choosing an engineering role strictly based on the highest Total Compensation (TC) is a common mistake that leads to burnout and career stagnation. 

As a senior leader, I advise candidates to evaluate offers across four dimensions: Compensation, Trajectory, Culture, and Engineering Excellence.

## 1. Deconstructing the Compensation Package

Not all dollars are created equal.
*   **Public Company Equity (RSUs):** This is essentially cash, subject to vesting schedules. Understand the vesting cliff (usually 1 year) and the vesting frequency afterward (monthly vs. quarterly).
*   **Startup Equity (Options):** This is lottery money. Do not value startup options at their "projected" value. Value them at $0 in your financial planning. Ask for the strike price, the current 409A valuation, and what percentage of the company your grant represents.
*   **Sign-on Bonuses:** These are great, but remember they are a one-time payout. A higher base salary compounds every year with raises.

## 2. Career Trajectory & Scope

Will this role make your *next* job hunt easier?
*   Are you being hired to maintain a legacy system, or to build greenfield architecture?
*   What is the promotion velocity? Ask the hiring manager: "Can you give me an example of an engineer on your team who was promoted recently, and what they did to achieve that?"
*   Will you have a manager who actively sponsors your growth, or just someone who assigns Jira tickets?

## 3. Engineering Excellence

You become the average of the engineers around you.
*   **Tech Stack:** Is it modern, or are you pigeonholing yourself into a dying technology?
*   **Practices:** Do they have CI/CD? Are code reviews rigorous or rubber-stamped? How is on-call structured? (A high-paying job with a toxic on-call rotation is not worth the stress).

## 4. Manager and Team Culture

The old adage is true: People leave managers, not companies.
*   Did the manager seem technically competent during the interview? 
*   Did they show empathy? 
*   Ask to meet the team without the manager present before you sign. This is the best way to get the unvarnished truth about the engineering culture.

Use a weighted matrix to score your offers. Assign a weight (1-5) to what matters most to you right now (e.g., Cash = 5, WLB = 4, Tech Stack = 3) and score each company. The math will often reveal what your gut already knows.
