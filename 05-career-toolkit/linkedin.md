# LinkedIn Optimization

Your resume is a static document you send out. LinkedIn is an inbound marketing engine. When your profile is optimized, recruiters and hiring managers will come to you, bypassing the traditional application queue entirely.

## The "Above the Fold" Experience

When I land on your profile, I see three things immediately: your photo, your headline, and your About section. 

1.  **The Photo:** Professional, well-lit, approachable. You don't need a suit, but you shouldn't be holding a beer either.
2.  **The Headline:** Stop using "Software Engineer at Company X". Use this real estate to tell me your value proposition.
    *   *Example:* "Senior Backend Engineer | Scaling Distributed Systems in Go & Python | Ex-Stripe"
3.  **The About Section:** Write a brief narrative. Who are you, what problems do you enjoy solving, and what is your engineering philosophy? Show some personality.

## Experience & Keyword Density

Recruiters use LinkedIn Recruiter, which relies heavily on search algorithms. If you want to be found for "Kubernetes" or "React", those words need to appear multiple times in your profile.

*   Expand on your job descriptions similarly to your resume, but you have more room to add context about the team size and business domain.
*   List specific projects and the tech stack used for each.

## Engagement Signals

Hiring managers like to see engineers who are engaged in the craft.
*   **Recommendations:** Get 2-3 recommendations from former managers or senior peers. This provides massive social proof.
*   **Activity:** You don't need to be an influencer, but sharing an interesting technical article or commenting thoughtfully on a peer's post shows you are active in the community.
*   **Open to Work:** Use the "Open to Work" feature, but you can set it to "Recruiters only" if you want to keep your search discreet.
