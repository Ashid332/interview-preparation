# Salary Negotiation

Many engineers leave tens of thousands of dollars on the table because they treat salary negotiation as an adversarial conflict rather than a collaborative business discussion. As an engineering manager, I expect senior candidates to negotiate. It shows you understand your market value.

## The Golden Rule: Information Asymmetry

The company has more information than you. They know the budget, the pay bands, and what everyone else makes. Your goal in the early stages of interviewing is to **delay giving a number** for as long as possible.

*   **Recruiter:** "What are your salary expectations?"
*   **You:** "Right now, I'm focused on finding a role where I can make a strong technical impact and grow. I'm sure if we find there's a mutual fit, we can agree on a competitive package. Could you share the budget approved for this role?"

## Total Compensation (TC)

Never negotiate just the base salary. Engineering compensation is usually a three-legged stool:
1.  **Base Salary:** The cash you get every paycheck. (Usually has strict internal bands).
2.  **Bonus:** Performance-based cash (often a percentage of base).
3.  **Equity (RSUs or Options):** Ownership in the company.

Often, a company cannot budge on base salary due to internal equity rules, but they have massive flexibility on the equity grant or a sign-on bonus. If base is capped, ask for a higher sign-on bonus or more stock.

## Leverage: The Best BATNA

In negotiation, your power comes from your BATNA (Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement). Your best alternative is **another job offer**.
Having competing offers is the single most effective way to increase your compensation. Companies will often break their internal bands to match a competing offer from a top-tier tech company.

## The Script: How to Counter-Offer

Always express excitement, but indicate that the offer isn't quite there yet.

> "I am incredibly excited about the team and the technical challenges we discussed. This is absolutely where I want to be. However, based on the market data I'm seeing for my skillset and [mention competing offer if you have one], I was expecting a Total Compensation closer to $X. If you can get to $X, I am ready to sign today."

Notice the ending: *"I am ready to sign today."* This lowers the risk for the recruiter. They know that if they fight to get you that money, they close the req.
