# Networking for Engineers

Engineers often view networking as a transactional, sleazy activity involving handing out business cards at awkward mixers. In reality, effective networking is simply about building long-term, mutually beneficial relationships with other people who love building software.

The best time to build your network is when you don't need a job.

## Shift Your Mindset

Stop thinking, "How can this person help me get a job?" 
Start thinking, "What interesting things is this person working on, and how can I add value to their professional life?"

## Tactical Networking

### 1. The "Value-First" Approach
When reaching out to someone, offer value before asking for a favor. 
*   Share an article relevant to their recent work.
*   Point out a typo in their documentation (politely) and offer a PR.
*   Congratulate them on a recent launch and ask a highly specific technical question about how they built it.

### 2. Internal Networking
If you are currently employed, your best network is right next to you.
*   Talk to PMs, designers, and engineers on other teams. 
*   When colleagues leave for new companies, stay in touch. In two years, they will be your fast track to a referral at a top-tier tech company.

### 3. Communities and Meetups
Find your tribe.
*   **Discord/Slack Communities:** Join communities specific to your stack (e.g., Elixir Slack, Reactiflux). Be helpful. Answer questions.
*   **Tech Twitter/LinkedIn:** Engage meaningfully. Don't just self-promote; amplify others' work.
*   **Local Meetups:** Attend local tech meetups. Volunteer to give a 5-minute lightning talk on a bug you recently fixed. Speaking instantly elevates your status in the room.

## The Long Game

Follow up. If someone gives you advice, take it, try it out, and email them a month later saying, "Hey, I tried X based on your advice, and Y happened. Thank you!" People love knowing their advice was actually useful.
