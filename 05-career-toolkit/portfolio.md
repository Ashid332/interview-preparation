# Portfolio Development

While a GitHub profile shows *how* you build, a portfolio shows *what* you build and the user experience you deliver. Portfolios are practically mandatory for frontend and full-stack engineers, but can also be highly beneficial for backend and data engineers if structured correctly.

## Showcasing Architecture and Impact

A good engineering portfolio shouldn't just be a list of screenshots. Treat each project as a mini case study. When I look at your portfolio, I want to understand your decision-making process.

For each major project, include:
1.  **The Problem:** What were you trying to solve?
2.  **The Architecture:** A high-level overview of the system design (consider embedding a simple Mermaid diagram).
3.  **Technical Decisions:** Why did you choose Postgres over MongoDB? Why Redis? Defend your choices.
4.  **The Outcome/Impact:** Did people use it? What did you learn? Did you hit any scaling bottlenecks?

## Tailoring for Your Specialty

*   **Frontend Engineers:** Your portfolio *is* the test. The site itself must have flawless responsive design, perfect accessibility (a11y), and score high on Lighthouse performance audits. 
*   **Backend Engineers:** Your portfolio might be a collection of API documentation, system architecture diagrams, or technical blog posts about difficult scaling problems you solved.
*   **Data Engineers:** Showcase data pipelines, ETL architectures, and perhaps dashboards built on top of complex queries.
