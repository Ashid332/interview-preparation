# Personal Branding for Engineers

"Personal branding" sounds like marketing jargon, but for engineers, it simply means building a public track record of your expertise. When you are a known entity, opportunities find you.

As a manager, if I google a candidate's name and find thoughtful blog posts about distributed systems, or conference talks on frontend performance, my confidence in them skyrockets before the interview even begins.

## The Triad of Engineering Brand

You don't need to be active everywhere. Pick one or two channels and be consistent.

### 1. Writing (The Highest ROI)
Writing forces you to clarify your technical thinking.
*   **Start a Blog:** Use Substack, Dev.to, or Medium. Better yet, build your own simple static site.
*   **What to Write About:** Write about bugs you fixed, architectural decisions you made, or tutorials on new technologies. Don't worry if it's been written about before; it hasn't been written from *your* perspective.
*   **The "Learn in Public" Ethos:** You don't have to be an expert. Document your journey. "How I'm learning Rust" is just as valuable as "Advanced Rust Concurrency."

### 2. Speaking
Public speaking establishes immense credibility.
*   Start small: Give a 10-minute talk at an internal company lunch-and-learn.
*   Graduate to local meetups.
*   Eventually, submit CFPs (Call for Papers) to regional or national conferences. 

### 3. Open Source & Community
(See the [Open Source](open-source.md) guide for deep details). Being a maintainer or core contributor to a widely used library is one of the strongest brand signals in the industry.

## Consistency Over Virality

Don't try to write the ultimate viral tech article. Focus on consistency. Publish one short, high-quality piece of content a month. Over a few years, this compounds into a massive library of proof regarding your technical depth and communication skills.

Communication is a senior-level skill. Building a brand is essentially proving your communication skills publicly.
