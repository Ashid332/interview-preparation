# Cold Outreach

Cold outreach (messaging someone you don't know) is a superpower if done correctly. If done poorly, it's just spam. As a hiring manager, I receive dozens of cold LinkedIn messages a week. I ignore 95% of them. The 5% I respond to have a specific anatomy.

## Why Cold Outreach Works

Applying through a company's career portal puts you in a database with hundreds of other applicants. Reaching out directly to the hiring manager or a senior engineer on the team bypasses the queue and forces a human to look at your profile.

## The Anatomy of a Perfect Cold Message

Your message must be short (under 100 words), highly customized, and have a low-friction "Ask".

1.  **The Hook:** Show that you've done your homework. Reference a recent blog post they wrote, a talk they gave, or a specific technical challenge their company is facing.
2.  **The Value Pitch:** One sentence about who you are and the specific impact you've had that aligns with their needs. (Use the XYZ formula from your resume).
3.  **The Ask (Call to Action):** Never ask for a job. Ask for a brief conversation, or better yet, just ask a specific question that they can answer asynchronously.

### Example of a Bad Cold Message
> "Hi [Name], I am a software engineer looking for a job. I saw your company is hiring. Can you review my resume and get me an interview? Thanks."
*(Result: Immediate delete. You've given me work to do.)*

### Example of a Great Cold Message
> "Hi [Name], I loved your recent engineering blog post on how you migrated from REST to GraphQL. I recently led a similar migration at [Company], reducing payload size by 30%. I saw you're hiring for a Senior Backend Engineer. I've applied online, but would you be open to a quick 10-minute chat about the engineering culture on your team? No worries if you're too busy."
*(Result: I will click your profile. If you look good, I will route you directly to a recruiter.)*

## Who to Target

1.  **Hiring Managers:** Target the Engineering Manager or Director of Engineering. They feel the pain of the empty seat the most.
2.  **Internal Recruiters:** They are incentivized to find good candidates.
3.  **Engineers on the Team:** They can offer a referral and give you the inside scoop on the tech stack.
