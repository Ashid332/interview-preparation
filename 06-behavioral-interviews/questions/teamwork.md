# Teamwork

## Why Interviewers Ask This
Software engineering is a collaborative discipline. Interviewers want to know how you operate within a team, how you support colleagues, how you handle diverse working styles, and whether you put the team's success above your personal ego.

## Evaluation Criteria

| Criteria | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Collaboration** | Does the candidate actively seek input and work well with others? |
| **Supportiveness** | Do they help unblock peers and contribute to a positive culture? |
| **Communication** | Can they articulate complex ideas clearly to team members? |
| **Flexibility** | Are they willing to compromise for the good of the team? |

## Weak Answer
"I'm a great team player. In my last job, I always did my assigned tickets on time. I didn't talk much in meetings because I preferred to just put my head down and code, but I never caused any problems."
*Critique: Passive approach to teamwork. Simply doing your job in isolation isn't teamwork.*

## Average Answer
"Our team had a huge deadline, and one of the junior developers was struggling with their part of the feature. I stayed late one evening to help them debug their code so we could get the release out on time."
*Critique: Good example of helping out, but it's a bit one-dimensional and reactive.*

## Strong Answer (STAR)
**Situation**: "Our cross-functional team was tasked with building a new analytics dashboard. The frontend engineers and backend engineers were working in silos, which led to mismatched API expectations and constant integration delays."
**Task**: "I realized we needed a better way to collaborate to meet our launch deadline."
**Action**: "I initiated a weekly cross-team alignment meeting. I also created a shared swagger documentation repository where we defined the API contracts together before writing any code. When the frontend team hit a roadblock with state management, I organized a pair-programming session to help them optimize the data fetching, even though I'm primarily a backend developer."
**Result**: "By establishing clear contracts and breaking down the silos, we cut our integration time in half. We launched the dashboard a week early, and the shared documentation process became a standard practice for all new features in our department."

## Follow-Up Questions
* "Tell me about a time you had to work with a difficult team member."
* "How do you handle a situation where a team member is not pulling their weight?"
* "Describe a time you had to compromise on a technical approach for the sake of the team."

## Hiring Manager Notes
I look for "force multipliers"—engineers who not only do their own work well but make everyone around them better. Active collaboration, knowledge sharing, and empathy are key indicators of a strong team player.

## Scoring Rubric

| Rating | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1 - Poor** | Operates in a silo, takes credit for team success, or complains about colleagues. |
| **2 - Fair** | Does their share of the work but doesn't actively elevate others. |
| **3 - Good** | Collaborates effectively, helps peers when asked, communicates well. |
| **4 - Excellent** | Proactively improves team processes, mentors others, bridges gaps between disciplines. |

## Practice Exercise
Think of a time when the team's success was at risk. Write down your STAR response, highlighting how your specific actions brought the team together or improved the collective output.
