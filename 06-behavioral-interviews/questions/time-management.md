# Time Management

## Why Interviewers Ask This
Engineers rarely work on just one thing at a time. Interviewers want to see how you prioritize tasks, manage competing deadlines, handle context switching, and communicate delays when necessary.

## Evaluation Criteria

| Criteria | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Prioritization** | How does the candidate decide what is most important? |
| **Organization** | Do they use systems or tools to manage their workload? |
| **Communication** | Do they proactively inform stakeholders of risks or delays? |
| **Adaptability** | Can they handle shifting priorities without getting overwhelmed? |

## Weak Answer
"I just work on whatever my manager tells me to do first. Sometimes I get too many tickets, and I just have to work weekends to get them all done. I usually manage to finish everything eventually."
*Critique: Shows poor boundary setting, lack of independent prioritization skills, and reliance on brute force (working weekends) rather than efficient management.*

## Average Answer
"I had two projects due the same week. I realized I couldn't do both, so I prioritized the one that was for a bigger client. I worked hard and got that one done on time, but the other one was a few days late. I told my manager afterwards."
*Critique: Made a prioritization choice, but failed to communicate the delay proactively, which is a major red flag.*

## Strong Answer (STAR)
**Situation**: "During our Q3 launch week, I was leading the backend integration for a critical feature when a high-severity bug was discovered in an older system I maintained."
**Task**: "I had to resolve the production incident immediately without jeopardizing the launch timeline for the new feature."
**Action**: "First, I triaged the bug and estimated it would take a full day to fix. I immediately alerted my product manager and tech lead about the conflict. I proposed a plan: I would dedicate the next 24 hours entirely to the production bug. I asked a mid-level engineer on my team to take over my launch tasks for the day, providing him with a detailed handover doc and agreeing to review his PRs in the evening. This ensured neither task stalled."
**Result**: "I patched the production bug within 18 hours. Because of the clear delegation and proactive communication, the backend integration also stayed on track, and we successfully launched the new feature on schedule."

## Follow-Up Questions
* "How do you typically organize your daily and weekly tasks?"
* "Tell me about a time you completely missed a deadline. What happened?"
* "How do you handle scope creep on a project you are managing?"

## Hiring Manager Notes
Proactive communication is the most important element here. I want to know that if a candidate is falling behind, they will raise their hand early and propose a solution or ask for help, rather than hiding the problem until the deadline passes.

## Scoring Rubric

| Rating | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1 - Poor** | Misses deadlines constantly, fails to communicate delays, lacks organization. |
| **2 - Fair** | Manages time adequately but struggles with unexpected interruptions. |
| **3 - Good** | Prioritizes well, uses organizational tools, communicates risks. |
| **4 - Excellent** | Expertly balances competing demands, delegates effectively, and manages stakeholder expectations flawlessly. |

## Practice Exercise
Think about a time you had too much to do and not enough time. Outline your STAR response focusing on the criteria you used to prioritize and how you communicated your decisions to others.
