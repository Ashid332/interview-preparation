# Tell Me About Yourself

## Why Interviewers Ask This
This is often the opening question. Interviewers ask this to break the ice, understand your career trajectory, and see how well you can synthesize your experience into a coherent, engaging narrative. It sets the tone for the rest of the interview.

## Evaluation Criteria

| Criteria | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Clarity** | Is the narrative easy to follow and structured chronologically or thematically? |
| **Relevance** | Does the candidate highlight experience relevant to the target role? |
| **Brevity** | Is the answer concise (2-3 minutes) without rambling? |
| **Enthusiasm** | Does the candidate show passion for their work and the opportunity? |

## Weak Answer
"Hi, I'm John. I grew up in Ohio and went to college there. Then I got a job at Company A doing some QA work. After a while, I moved to Company B where I did full-stack development for 5 years. We worked on a lot of different projects. In my free time, I like playing video games and hiking. Now I'm looking for a new job."
*Critique: Too generic, lacks structure, doesn't highlight achievements or relevance to the role.*

## Average Answer
"I've been a software engineer for about 7 years. I started at Company A doing backend work in Java, building APIs. Then I moved to Company B where I led a small team and we migrated our monolith to microservices using AWS. It was a tough project but we got it done. Now I'm looking for a senior role where I can continue to lead projects and work on scalable systems, which is why I'm interested in this position."
*Critique: Better structure and mentions some achievements, but lacks a compelling "hook" and specific impact.*

## Strong Answer (STAR)
**Present**: "Currently, I'm a Senior Backend Engineer at TechCorp, where I lead a team of three building our core payment processing microservices using Go and Kubernetes."
**Past**: "Before this, I spent four years at FinStart, where I grew from a junior to mid-level engineer. My proudest achievement there was designing a caching layer that reduced API latency by 40%, which directly improved our checkout conversion rate."
**Future**: "While I enjoy my current role, I'm looking to join a company with a larger engineering organization where I can tackle complex distributed systems challenges at a higher scale. That's why I was so excited to see this opening on your infrastructure team, as it aligns perfectly with my background and career goals."

## Follow-Up Questions
* "Can you tell me more about that caching layer project you mentioned?"
* "What made you decide to transition from Company A to Company B?"
* "What has been the most challenging aspect of your current leadership role?"

## Hiring Manager Notes
A great answer provides a roadmap for the rest of the interview. I'm looking for candidates who can pitch themselves effectively, highlighting not just *what* they did, but the *impact* they had. The present-past-future structure is usually the most effective.

## Scoring Rubric

| Rating | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1 - Poor** | Rambling, irrelevant details, no clear structure, longer than 4 minutes. |
| **2 - Fair** | Basic chronological walkthrough of resume, lacks focus on achievements. |
| **3 - Good** | Structured answer, highlights relevant experience, mentions some impact. |
| **4 - Excellent** | Engaging narrative, clearly connects past experience to the open role, quantifies achievements, concise and confident. |

## Practice Exercise
Draft your "Tell me about yourself" pitch using the Present-Past-Future framework. Time yourself reading it aloud to ensure it falls between 90 and 150 seconds.
