# Adaptability

## Why Interviewers Ask This
The tech industry moves fast; requirements change, tools evolve, and reorganizations happen. Interviewers want to see that you are flexible, open to change, can learn quickly, and don't become paralyzed when plans shift unexpectedly.

## Evaluation Criteria

| Criteria | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Flexibility** | How willingly does the candidate embrace new approaches or changes? |
| **Learning Agility** | Can they quickly pick up new technologies or domains? |
| **Resilience** | Do they maintain a positive attitude in the face of disruption? |
| **Problem Solving** | Can they pivot their strategy when the original plan becomes obsolete? |

## Weak Answer
"We were halfway through building a feature in React when management decided we had to rewrite it in Angular because of a new company policy. I was really frustrated because I like React better. I complained about it a lot, but I eventually learned Angular and got it done."
*Critique: Shows resistance to change, a negative attitude, and inflexibility.*

## Average Answer
"Our project scope changed two weeks before the deadline. They wanted to add a whole new user flow. I had to scramble, but I learned the new requirements and worked some extra hours. We managed to get most of the new flow implemented."
*Critique: Shows some flexibility, but lacks a structured approach to handling the change, relying entirely on scrambling and extra hours.*

## Strong Answer (STAR)
**Situation**: "Six months into a year-long project building a custom analytics engine, our company acquired a startup that had a superior, ready-made analytics product."
**Task**: "Leadership decided to scrap our custom build and integrate the acquired product instead. I needed to pivot my team's focus entirely while maintaining morale, as we had invested heavily in the original project."
**Action**: "Instead of dwelling on the sunk cost, I organized a team meeting to acknowledge the disappointment but reframe the acquisition as an opportunity to ship value to customers faster. I led the effort to dive into the acquired startup's codebase. I mapped our existing data models to their schema and created an integration plan. I also set up knowledge-transfer sessions with their engineers to quickly upskill my team on their stack."
**Result**: "We successfully pivoted and completed the integration in three months—cutting the original project timeline in half. The team adapted quickly, and our integration became the standard architecture for future acquisitions."

## Follow-Up Questions
* "Tell me about a time you had to learn a new technology completely from scratch under a tight deadline."
* "How do you handle it when you strongly disagree with a change in direction from leadership?"
* "Describe a situation where a project you worked hard on was suddenly cancelled."

## Hiring Manager Notes
I'm looking for a positive, pragmatic attitude toward change. The best engineers recognize that code is just a means to an end (business value). I want to see candidates who can detach their ego from their work and pivot efficiently when the business needs them to.

## Scoring Rubric

| Rating | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1 - Poor** | Highly resistant to change, complains, or struggles to learn new things. |
| **2 - Fair** | Adapts eventually but with friction or a drop in performance. |
| **3 - Good** | Handles change professionally, learns quickly, and adjusts plans accordingly. |
| **4 - Excellent** | Champions change, helps others adapt, and finds strategic advantage in the new direction. |

## Practice Exercise
Identify a time when the "goalposts were moved" on a project you were working on. Write a STAR response detailing how you managed your own reaction and practically adjusted your work to meet the new goal.
