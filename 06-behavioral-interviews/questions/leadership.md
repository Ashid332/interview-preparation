# Leadership

## Why Interviewers Ask This
Interviewers ask this to identify candidates who take initiative, guide others, and drive projects to success, even without formal authority. They want to see if you can motivate a team and handle responsibility.

## Evaluation Criteria

| Criteria | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Initiative** | Did the candidate step up without being asked? |
| **Influence** | Were they able to guide and motivate others? |
| **Responsibility** | Did they take ownership of the outcome? |
| **Impact** | What was the result of their leadership? |

## Weak Answer
"In my last project, my manager was away, so I told everyone what to do. I assigned tasks and made sure they finished them on time. We got the project done, but people complained about the workload."
*Critique: Shows dictatorial behavior rather than true leadership. Lacks empathy and focuses only on task assignment.*

## Average Answer
"Our team was falling behind on a release. I stepped in and helped organize our Jira board. I ran the standups and made sure everyone knew what they were doing. We managed to release only a few days late."
*Critique: Shows some initiative but lacks details on how they motivated the team or overcame specific obstacles.*

## Strong Answer (STAR)
**Situation**: "During a major platform migration, our tech lead suddenly had to take medical leave. The team was disorganized, and morale was dropping due to impending deadlines."
**Task**: "We needed someone to coordinate the remaining migration tasks, unblock developers, and manage stakeholder expectations."
**Action**: "I volunteered to step in as interim lead. I organized a planning session to break down the remaining work, paired junior devs with seniors to accelerate learning, and set up daily syncs with the product manager to communicate realistic timelines. I focused on removing blockers and keeping the team motivated."
**Result**: "We successfully completed the migration on the revised schedule. The team felt supported, and my manager formally promoted me to a lead role shortly after they returned."

## Follow-Up Questions
* "How did you handle team members who were resistant to your leadership?"
* "What was the hardest part about stepping into that role?"
* "How do you balance leading with your own individual contributor work?"

## Hiring Manager Notes
True leadership is often demonstrated through influence rather than authority. I look for candidates who empower others, provide clarity in ambiguous situations, and take accountability for the team's success.

## Scoring Rubric

| Rating | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1 - Poor** | Exhibits bossy or micromanaging behavior; fails to achieve the goal. |
| **2 - Fair** | Steps up but struggles to motivate the team; mixed results. |
| **3 - Good** | Effectively organizes the team and achieves the goal; demonstrates basic leadership skills. |
| **4 - Excellent** | Inspires and empowers the team; overcomes significant challenges; delivers outstanding results. |

## Practice Exercise
Recall a situation where you had to lead a project or initiative. Write down your STAR response, focusing on how you influenced others and managed the process.
