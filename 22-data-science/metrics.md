# Product Metrics

## Overview
Understanding product metrics is essential for analytics. You must be able to define, track, and analyze KPIs (Key Performance Indicators) such as conversion rates, retention, churn, ARPU (Average Revenue Per User), and LTV (Lifetime Value).

## Interview Questions

### Q1: How do you define user retention for a social media app?
**Difficulty:** Medium | **Frequency:** High | **Companies:** TikTok, Pinterest, X

**Excellent Answer:**
Retention is usually defined as the percentage of users who return to the app after their initial sign-up or first action. For a social media app, I would define "return" not just as opening the app, but as a meaningful action, like viewing a post, liking, or commenting. 
I would look at **N-day retention** (e.g., D1, D7, D30) to see if users come back on specific days, and **bracket retention** to see if they return within specific time frames. The exact time window depends on the expected natural frequency of use; for a social app, daily or weekly is standard.

**Common Mistakes:**
- Defining retention purely as "login" without considering core actions.
- Using the same retention definition for a daily app (social media) and a monthly app (travel booking).

## Real-World Applications
- Analyzing why D7 retention is high but D30 retention falls off a cliff.
- Creating a North Star metric that aligns all company departments.

## Hiring Manager Perspective
A good analyst thinks like a product manager. They don't just calculate a number; they question whether it's the *right* number. If engagement goes up but revenue goes down, they know how to investigate the trade-off.
