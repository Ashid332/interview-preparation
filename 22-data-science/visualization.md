# Data Visualization

## Overview
Data visualization is about storytelling. Interviews test your ability to choose the right chart for the right data, design intuitive dashboards, and communicate insights clearly without misleading the audience.

## Interview Questions

### Q1: When would you use a scatter plot versus a line chart?
**Difficulty:** Easy | **Frequency:** High | **Companies:** Tableau, Bloomberg, McKinsey

**Excellent Answer:**
I use a **line chart** to show trends over time for continuous data. The connecting lines imply a progression from one point to the next, which is perfect for time series (e.g., monthly revenue over a year).
I use a **scatter plot** to show the relationship or correlation between two numerical variables. It helps identify clusters, outliers, and correlations (e.g., plotting marketing spend vs. customer acquisition cost for various campaigns).

**Common Mistakes:**
- Using line charts for categorical data on the x-axis.
- Using pie charts when there are too many categories (bar charts are better).

## Real-World Applications
- Designing a funnel visualization to show where users drop out during checkout.
- Using a heat map to show user activity by day of the week and hour of the day.

## Hiring Manager Perspective
I look for empathy for the end-user. Does the candidate understand that the CEO has 5 seconds to look at this chart? They need to highlight the main takeaway clearly (using titles, annotations, and color sparingly) rather than dumping data on a screen.
